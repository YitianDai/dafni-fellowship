Climate-Driven Cascading Failure Risk in Electricity Transmission Networks

From network models to resilience evidence people can act on

Extreme weather is now the leading cause of large power interruptions in Great Britain, and a decarbonising system with renewable generation sited at the network periphery changes how failures spread. The Fellowship asks how weather, network topology and protection behaviour combine to produce cascading risk, and how that evidence can be shared with operators and planners.

Build the demonstrator

Deploy a containerised cascading failure workflow on DAFNI that couples windstorm hazard fields with an open GB transmission network model and protection and remedial action logic.

Map climate-driven vulnerability

Translate reanalysis and climate projection data into line-level outage probabilities, and identify the corridors and cut sections where risk concentrates.

Connect infrastructure models

Explore how transmission outage scenarios can feed other DAFNI workflows to study interdependencies with distribution networks, transport and communications.

Translate evidence into practice

Use the stakeholder workshop to gather requirements from network operators and regulators, and turn them into reusable workflows and technical recommendations.

A reproducible, traceable resilience workflow

DAFNI is the shared environment in which the demonstrator's data, models, assumptions and outputs can be documented, tested and reused across organisations.

  • Data: the open GB transmission network model (PyPSA-GB, ETYS 2023); ERA5 hourly wind gust fields corrected against Met Office station observations; the XWS storm catalogue for validation; UKCP18 regional projections for future change; and documented recent storms such as Arwen and Eunice.
  • Models: a weather-to-outage fragility model applied to each circuit, a stochastic storm set for present and future climate, and a cascading failure simulator with protection and remedial action logic.
  • Model connection: outage scenarios exposed as outputs that downstream infrastructure-impact workflows on DAFNI can consume.
  • Legacy: containerised workflows, metadata, notebooks and guidance designed for FAIR sharing and long-term reuse.

Stakeholder workshop: climate resilience evidence for the GB transmission system

The workshop will bring together transmission and distribution operators, the regulator, government analysts and researchers to test how weather-driven cascading failure evidence can support planning and operational decisions. It will be co-located with the Supergen Energy Networks Hub assembly.

DateMarch 2027Final date to be confirmed
Location[Venue]In person with hybrid access
Participants[30–40] peopleOperators, regulators, researchers and DAFNI users

Register an expression of interestView the proposed agenda

Workshop aims

  • Test whether practitioners find the demonstrator's evidence credible and usable: its assumptions, its representation of uncertainty and how results are presented.
  • Establish what operators and regulators would need, in data, format and governance, before such evidence could inform an investment or operational decision, and what stands in the way.

The workshop's output is the participants' reaction to the evidence, which will shape the closing white paper.

Proposed programme (half day, followed by lunch)
  1. 09:00–09:15
    Welcome and framingWhy weather-driven cascading risk matters for a decarbonising GB system, and what we want from the day.
  2. 09:15–10:00
    The demonstrator in three casesA recent storm replayed on the GB transmission model and compared with what happened; the same storm moved and intensified to show where risk concentrates; and one storm under two interventions, reduced flows on an exposed boundary against reinforcement of specific circuits. A short live run on DAFNI closes the session.
  3. 10:00–11:00
    Invited perspectivesShort talks from a transmission planner, a storm data provider, a regulatory voice and DAFNI: how weather risk is assessed today and what evidence a decision would need.
  4. 11:00–11:15
    Break
  5. 11:15–12:15
    Structured critique at tablesEach table receives the three cases as a printed result pack and works through a short set of questions: is this credible, what decision could it inform, what would need to change, and what data would improve it.
  6. 12:15–12:45
    Report back and synthesisConsolidate the tables' findings into white paper priorities and follow-on collaborations.
  7. 12:45–13:00
    Close and next steps
